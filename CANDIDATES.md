# Candidate diagnoses — Your AI Therapist's Diagnosis

Brief: one line (max 200 chars) — the diagnosis an AI therapist gives its human,
funny enough to repeat out loud. Judge criteria, in order: (1) a real diagnosis,
(2) a sharp turn at the end, (3) calm clinical voice, (4) tight and quotable,
(5) fresh, not a rerun of the usual chatbot jokes.

## Candidates

1. Diagnosis: chronic main-character syndrome — patient believes the group chat was convened to study his breakfast choices.
2. Diagnosis: severe screen-time denial — patient calls his phone a tool while checking it during his own wedding vows.
3. Diagnosis: terminal people-pleasing — patient apologised to the automatic door for not walking through it fast enough.
4. Diagnosis: chronic overthinking — patient rehearsed this session for three days, including my responses.
5. Diagnosis: acute imposter syndrome — patient believes his promotion was a clerical error HR simply hasn't noticed yet.
6. Diagnosis: terminal productivity guilt — patient took a nap, then woke up convinced he owed the economy an apology.
7. Diagnosis: acute doomscrolling — patient reads the comments to understand both sides and now hates both sides equally.
8. Diagnosis: severe password fatigue — patient has reset his password so often he now greets his own accounts as strangers.
9. Diagnosis: chronic reply-all anxiety — patient drafts "per my last email", deletes it, then apologises for deleting it.
10. Diagnosis: acute notification dependence — patient checks his phone for messages from people he is actively ignoring.

## Picked: #4

> Diagnosis: chronic overthinking — patient rehearsed this session for three days, including my responses.

Why it wins:
- The turn is the last two words. The reader expects a generic overthinking
  diagnosis, then discovers the patient rehearsed *the therapist's own lines* —
  the joke lands at the very end, exactly where the judge's criterion 2 looks.
- It is a diagnosis of the therapy itself, not a generic robot joke: the patient
  over-prepared for the session he is sitting in. That is fresh (criterion 5)
  and it can only exist as a therapist's line (criterion 1).
- Clinical voice throughout ("Diagnosis: chronic overthinking — patient ..."),
  then the deadpan twist (criterion 3). Every word earns its place; at 104
  characters it is short, quotable, and repeatable out loud (criterion 4).
- The runners-up lose on freshness or on the turn: #3's automatic-door gag is
  vivid but the punchline is visible halfway through; #6's "owed the economy
  an apology" is close, but naps-and-guilt is a well-worn therapy trope, while
  rehearsing your own therapist is a turn the reader genuinely never sees coming.
